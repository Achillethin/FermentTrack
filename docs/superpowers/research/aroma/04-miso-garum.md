Modelling hints from the sources (not parameters, for the template-calibration pass):
- HEMF needs **both** yeast and a koji-derived pentose-Maillard precursor: no HEMF without added/native yeast
  ([M9] Fig. 1; [M10] Table 1), none without soybean ([M1]); precursor is itself unstable (peaks at ~2 weeks of aging
  at 37 °C, later at lower T; [M1] Fig. 5). Warm (30–37 °C) → fast rise to a peak at 30–60 d then decline; 25 °C →
  slower rise, later peak (120 d), similar or higher end value ([M8] Fig. 3, [M9] Fig. 4).
- HEMF chemical loss with yeast stopped: ≈ 0.03 d⁻¹ at 30 °C, ≈ 0.008 d⁻¹ at 20 °C, ≈ 0 at 5–10 °C ([M9] Table 3; est.).
- Ehrlich alcohols peak at 60–90 d then fall 20–60 % by 120–180 d ([M2] Table 3, [M8] Fig. 1) → a loss term is needed.

**Trimethylamine note.** [G2] attributes TMA to degradation of TMAO "related to bacterial decay"; it appeared only
at 7 months (0 at 3 months) in natural and koji sauces and was lower with 35 °C heat-held fermentation. None of
the model's organisms is documented here as a TMAO reducer (not checked for *T. halophilus*; see E). Proposal:
model TMA as release from a fish TMAO precursor pool with a wide prior on the rate, flagged "producer not
modelled", or keep it out of series sums until a TMAO-reducing organism exists.
