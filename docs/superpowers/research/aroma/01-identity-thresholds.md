# 01 — Identity, descriptors and odour thresholds in water (table A)

Slice of the aroma literature review (see `BRIEF.md`). Covers the ~85 candidate odorants of spec § 4.2 plus acetic acid and ethanol. Status: **complete for this pass** (2026-10-05). 85 compounds; see the summary after table A.

## Method

**Identity.** For every compound the PubChem CID was resolved twice through the PUG REST API, by name and by CAS number; both lookups returned the same CID unless noted. The CAS, ChEBI and KEGG IDs come from the PubChem RegistryID cross-references, then checked one by one: each ChEBI ID against its label in the EBI OLS4 ChEBI ontology, each KEGG C-number against its KEGG entry (`rest.kegg.jp/get/…`, NAME and CAS fields). Cross-references whose KEGG entry is a different compound were removed: citronellol → C21980, trimethylpyrazine → C10351, decanoic acid → C17870, geranial → C15604/C34910. A "—" in the KEGG column means neither PubChem nor a KEGG name search (`rest.kegg.jp/find/compound/<name>`) gave an entry. Stereo-specific odorants carry the CID of the stereoisomer: (Z)-3-hexenal, (Z)-3-hexenol, (E)-2-nonenal, (Z)-4-heptenal, (E)-β-damascenone, (E)-β-ionone, geranial and neral. Linalool, citronellol, carvone and limonene carry the stereo-unspecified CID, and their enantiomers' thresholds are given separately in the notes.

**Thresholds.** These are orthonasal **detection** thresholds in water, in µg/kg (≈ µg/L). Three sources were opened, in this order:

1. **[CZ] Czerny et al. 2008, Table 1** (full text opened). This is a re-determination for 84 key food odorants by one protocol: a panel of 13–22 assessors, 3-alternative forced-choice (3-AFC) triangle series diluted 1:3 in tap water, each compound's purity checked by GC-olfactometry, and the panel threshold taken as the geometric mean of individual thresholds. Table 1 gives the recognition threshold (R), the detection threshold (D) and a "threshold range in literature" column (*lit*) with the original citations. *lit* is secondary, so it goes into the **range** only, never into the median. Table 2 repeats three acids.
2. **[LSB] Leibniz-LSB@TUM Odorant Database v1.2** (Kreissl et al. 2022). Its thresholds are determined "according to Eur Food Res Technol 2008, 228, 265" (user guide), so they follow the CZ protocol. Two kinds of entry were used. *basic* is the basic-table pair TW (detection) / TE (recognition) in water. *row X* is a substance-details literature row in which publication X reports the threshold; these are cited *as given by the database*, and the primary paper X was not opened. Rows that repeat the CZ value are not counted again. Only individual compound pages were consulted, and the database's citation terms are followed.
3. **[LEF] Leffingwell & Associates, "Odor & Flavor Detection Thresholds in Water (in parts per billion)"**. This is an older compilation (mostly Buttery, Guadagni, Takeoka and the ASTM DS 48A list); its reference numbers are given. A range from LEF counts once in the median, at its geometric midpoint, and both ends go into the range.

Where none of these three sources had a value in water, further sources were searched and are named in the row: Schwab 2013 for norfuraneol, Marcinkowska & Jeleń 2020 for isothiocyanates, Boelens & van Gemert 1993 for sulfur compounds, and beer and garlic papers. Descriptors for compounds without a CZ or LSB descriptor come from Flavornet.

**Median** is the median over distinct sources of detection thresholds in water. With an even number of sources it is the geometric mean of the two middle values, which suits the log-normal threshold prior of spec § 4.1. **Range** is the minimum–maximum over every water detection value found, including the CZ *lit* column, and excludes recognition thresholds. Values in other matrices (air, oil, starch, beer, wine model, water/ethanol) are listed in *notes* and never mixed into the median or range. Values that are suspect but kept are flagged in *notes*.

**Descriptors** use the Munich "aroma language": CZ Table 1 where the compound is there, otherwise the LSB odour-quality field, which comes from the same school and vocabulary. Other descriptor sources are named. The **series** column assigns 1–2 terms from the brief's list, based on the descriptor.

**Caveats for the model.** (i) For acids (acetic, butanoic, the branched and medium-chain fatty acids) and amines (trimethylamine) only the neutral species is volatile, so a threshold in neutral water understates the threshold at ferment pH for acids and overstates it for amines (see the trimethylamine row). (ii) Values from the Munich school dominate. When CZ and LEF differ by more than ~10×, the prior should be wide, not centred on CZ.

## Summary of table A

- **85 compounds** (83 candidates + acetic acid + ethanol). All have a verified PubChem CID. 84 have a ChEBI ID; raphasatin (4-methylthio-3-butenyl isothiocyanate) has none. 52 have a KEGG ID.
- **76 have a water detection threshold from at least one source that was opened.** 41 of these have a Czerny 2008 re-determination (CZ). The rest rest on the LSB database (same protocol family), Leffingwell compilations or a secondary review.
- **Thin evidence**, where the value comes from a single source or only a compiled or secondary value:
  - single source: 2-methylbutanol, methionol, 2-phenylethyl acetate, ethyl octanoate, 2,3-pentanedione, 4-ethylphenol, δ-dodecalactone, 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline;
  - one LSB basic-table value whose primary paper is unnamed: ethyl decanoate, 3-octanol;
  - only an older compilation (LEF): ethyl lactate, 2-pentylfuran, methyl salicylate, 2-heptanone, 2-nonanone;
  - only a secondary review range: norfuraneol.
- **More than 10× disagreement between sources**, so the prior should be wide: 2-methylpropanol, ethyl acetate, octanoic acid, decanoic acid (probable unit error in LSB), 1-octen-3-ol, methanethiol, (Z)-3-hexenol, geraniol, **(E)-β-ionone (170×)**, 4-ethylguaiacol, trimethylpyrazine, sotolon, **acetic acid (18×)**, ethanol.
- **No water threshold found: 9 compounds**, which are drop candidates for the OAV output unless table E leads give a value: 2,3-butanediol, allyl isothiocyanate (only the class range 5–200 µg/L for 19 ITCs), allyl cyanide, 3-butenyl isothiocyanate, 4-methylthio-3-butenyl isothiocyanate (Boelens & van Gemert 1993 explicitly give "unknown"), S-methyl thioacetate (only a beer flavour threshold of 50 µg/L), diallyl disulfide, allyl methyl disulfide and zingiberene. The glucosinolate and garlic blocks are therefore the weakest part of the candidate list.
- **pH matters** for the acids and for trimethylamine (see the method note). The engine should apply a matrix/pH factor rather than use these water values directly.
